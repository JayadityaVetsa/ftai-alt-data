## AP: earnings dollars versus percentage margin

Q2 2026 AP revenue of $875.028m and cost of sales of $614.529m imply $260.499m gross profit, up 50.7% from $172.802m in Q2 2025. Adjusted EBITDA grew from $164.864m to $249.716m while its margin fell from 33.63% to 28.54%. Segment operating expenses fell from 1.83% to 1.26% of sales. All comparisons use the same segment and period.

The revenue-growth bridge adds $129.383m EBITDA at the old margin and subtracts a $44.531m combined margin/mix/cost residual. This is an arithmetic decomposition, not an identified causal mix effect. The filings support a larger workload and growing absolute earnings; they do not isolate heavy versus light work margins or prove stable contribution per comparable engine. Reported modules grew from 184 to 296, but module production is not a homogeneous count of engines sold.

The workload illustration shows how higher-priced heavy work can dilute percentage margins at unchanged profit per job. Prices, work mix and contribution are editable assumptions. It is not fitted evidence of FTAI's actual workscopes. Structural deterioration would appear as lower comparable-job contribution, more rework, longer inventory holding or weaker returns on capital after controlling for work mix.

Cash remains a necessary test. Company disclosures identify $407m H1 AP inventory working-capital use. Consolidated CFO was negative $265.3m and company adjusted FCF was $255m; neither is isolated AP recurring cash flow. Engine refurbishment and sales cross operating and investing classifications. No segment ROIC or inventory-turn improvement is claimed from a consolidated denominator. ## Power: demand exists; addressability must be modeled

The $1.465bn anchor generator-set order is direct commercial evidence. Named 2027 Microsoft/Crusoe and Frontier campus designs are screening leads with unresolved gas, primary permits and utility dates. At 50% load phasing, PUE 1.2, 115 MW known available supply and a 50% competing-supply assumption, the two-site model yields 564.1 MW open demand. At 25% capture, 10% derating and 10% reserve overhead, that corresponds to seven potential FTAI units. Low/high presets produce 152/1,660 MW open demand. All are conditional estimates, not identified orders, verified shortages or the whole market. A 12-month gap is assumed rather than observed.

GEV's global backlog supports scarcity but cannot be subtracted from US campus demand. Siemens' March 2026 script describes turbine capacity sold out until FY2028. Wärtsilä's October 5 282 MW order has 2028 equipment delivery and 2029 operation; its earlier Q1 Q&A still described some unsold 2027/28 slots. Competing contracts establish buyer spending and can also reduce the open opportunity. Site allocation and overlapping supplier contracts cannot be guessed.

Texas reliability gates are not a blanket grid-connection ban. Virginia's framework also proposes limits on on-site gas. Gas is not assumed cheapest in every complete-site design: fuel, heat rate, infrastructure, emissions control and the value of earlier operation determine economics.
