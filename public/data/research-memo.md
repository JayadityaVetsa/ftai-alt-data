## 1. The price of earlier electricity is measurable without inventing customer profits

An efficient turbine is not necessarily the best economic choice if it arrives later. Our model solves for the contribution per expected operating MW-month that offsets earlier capital deployment, operating costs and any fuel-efficiency disadvantage over a common discounted horizon.

At the illustrative defaults—100 MW installed, 10% site derating, 10% redundancy overhead, 95% availability, 85% utilization, $4/MMBtu LHV gas, readiness at months 6 versus 18, and a five-year horizon—the threshold is approximately **$66,598 per expected operating MW-month** against the competing turbine. The waiting-for-grid threshold is approximately **$89,145**; connection capital is excluded. At assumed customer contribution of $100,000, the modeled advantage versus the turbine is approximately $24.0 million. These are conditional calculations, not measured customer economics.

The GE LM2500XPRESS +G4 comparator's 8,669 Btu/kWh figure is a **50 Hz LHV manufacturer rating**, not a guaranteed Texas 60 Hz site result. Mod-1 heat rate, commercial prices, lead times and installed performance are assumptions. Gas pricing must use the same energy basis. The calculator separates equipment readiness from gas, permits and electrical site readiness.

**Financial implication:** evidence of a repeatable timing advantage could support pricing and repeat orders. It does not establish actual margins or market share. Derive a defensible ASP/margin from actual commercial evidence before using this as a valuation catalyst.

**Falsification:** both alternatives wait on the same site bottleneck; a competitor matches commissioning timing; delivered capacity or availability falls; fuel/maintenance costs rise sufficiently; customers' evidenced contribution is below the threshold.

## 2. Order headlines obscure earnings ownership and cash timing

Jereh's July filing describes a **$1.465 billion generator-set purchase order**, not a $1.465 billion FTAI revenue disclosure. The model separates FTAI turbine sales, packaging economics, equity-method profit, maintenance and capital/working-capital investment. Unverified JV ownership and profit default to zero. A September update reports receipt of a first advance without disclosing its percentage or FTAI entitlement. Original filings: [contract](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-07-23/1225436739.PDF), [update](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-09-05/1225550221.PDF).

The contract also distinguishes performance adjustment and delivery-delay compensation provisions, each with a stated per-equipment 10% cap. Their interaction and pass-through to FTAI are unverified. Model them separately. Final settlement timing and deliveries extending beyond October 2027 make accounting recognition and collection timing relevant to the pitch.

The base assumption of 80 turbine deliveries at $6 million ASP and 35% gross margin yields **$139.2 million operating EBITDA** including assumed service profit and operating costs. After assumed cash taxes, $80 million capital investment and $40 million working-capital growth, the operating cash proxy is **negative $6.64 million**. This is not a forecast, and it excludes JV distributions. The within-horizon proxy applies an explicitly assumed 65% fraction; it is not an accounting recognition schedule.

**Financial implication:** a long can be supported by de-risked production and cash conversion, but counting the entire JV order or treating an advance as earned profit overstates value. Test whether company disclosures support the separate economic layers and whether the market already anticipates them.

**Falsification:** transfer pricing leaves thin turbine margins, delivery/acceptance slips, adjustments exceed the expected margin buffer, investment consumes the advance, or orders do not repeat.

## Supporting investigations and limitations

**Permit fingerprints:** the initial 15-record Texas screen separates primary power, emergency backup, conditional announcements and tax registrations. Longhorn’s historical initial application describes 420 MW with competing OEMs (p. 33); the current technical application could not be acquired, so current configuration is unverified. CloudBurst's 1,200 MW announcement is conditional. No open, execution-qualified Mod-1-compatible capacity or explicit FTAI site attribution has been verified. Zero eligible MW is an evidence gap, not proof of zero demand. Tax registration dates are not commissioning dates. Capacity cannot be converted to FTAI orders without qualification and capture evidence. TCEQ generator details withheld as confidential remain unavailable.

**Engine allocation:** the FAA registry provides an exact engine-type-code screen and aircraft manufacture-age distribution. It cannot prove engine serial eligibility, engine age, retirement, available cores or purchase price. Power's modeled net value must exceed aviation/parts alternatives after acquisition, rejection yield, conversion, testing and working capital. Under the defaults, Power net value is $1.757 million per output versus $1.0 million aviation net value; approximately 143 candidates are needed for 100 outputs at 70% yield. Those are assumptions. Add verified fleet disclosures and eligible-core evidence before claiming a supply moat.

**Valuation:** SOTP inputs are independent team assumptions. Consolidated debt, cash, preferred equity and a diluted-share proxy are reconciled once from the June financial statements. Consolidated investment carrying value is an interim equity proxy, not a SCI fair-value conclusion. A verified dated share price is deliberately blank; enter it to reverse-solve implied Power earnings across aviation valuations. No substantiated price target or market-mispricing conclusion is claimed.
